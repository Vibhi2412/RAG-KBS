# RAG-KBS
Optimizing Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Domain-Specific Knowledge-Based Systems

Large language models (LLMs) gives confident and polished answers that may not be always correct, which restricts their use in domain-specific knowledge-based systems. Retrieval augmented generation (RAG) provides responses from information retrieved from supporting documents, yet many studies do not report uncertainty measures or complete retrieval statistics. This study introduces a fully local RAG framework built from medical textbook collection containing 125,847 chunks. The system combines dense vector embeddings with Best Matching 25(BM25) keyword retrieval using Reciprocal Rank Fusion(RRF) which reranks the retrieved candidates with cross-encoder and generates evidence-based answers with locally deployed LLM through Ollama. The workflow is defined as an eleven step algorithm. Evaluation has three stages i.e. retrieval testing on 150 corpus-based questions, answer-quality assessment on 40 questions and accuracy analysis of 100 MedQA-USMLE benchmark questions. Hybrid retrieval with reranking achieved Hit@5 of 0.887 and mean reciprocal rank of 0.825, compared with 0.827 and 0.743 for dense retrieval alone. Reranking improved Hit@1 from 0.700 to 0.780. Measured answer correctness varied depending on the evaluation method, reaching 0.700 with a strict automated judge, 0.900 under rubric-based assessment, and 0.950 to 0.975 after manual review, while faithfulness of 0.950. On the MedQA benchmark, accuracy rose from 0.53 without retrieval to 0.60 with retrieval (95% confidence intervals: 0.433 to 0.625 and 0.502 to 0.691).
